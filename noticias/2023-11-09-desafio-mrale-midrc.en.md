MALTA Lab placed **4th in the mRALE challenge**, run by the Medical Imaging and Data Resource Center (MIDRC). The competition evaluated artificial intelligence models that predict the severity of COVID-19 from chest X-rays, and the result earned the lab a US$5,000 prize.

MALTA was the only Latin American institution among the winners. The challenge was funded by the National Institute of Biomedical Imaging and Bioengineering (NIBIB), hosted at the University of Chicago, and run with the American College of Radiology (ACR), the Radiological Society of North America (RSNA), and the American Association of Physicists in Medicine (AAPM).

> It's an impressive result, and it makes me very proud.
>
> — Rodrigo Coelho Barros, MALTA coordinator (translated from Portuguese)

## The method

The solution uses semi-supervised learning to predict the mRALE score, which measures how much of the lungs is affected on the X-ray. The work was accepted as an oral presentation at SPIE Medical Imaging 2024: [Semi-Supervised Learning for mRALE Score Prediction in COVID-19 Chest Radiographs](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.3003750).

> Our work aims not only to advance the technological state of the art, but also to promote fairness and social well-being through data science.
>
> — Christian Mattjie, MALTA PhD student (translated from Portuguese)

## More work in medical imaging

Around the same time, the group had two papers accepted at the 23rd IEEE BIBE: an [evaluation of the Segment Anything Model (SAM) in 2D medical imaging](https://doi.org/10.1109/BIBE60311.2023.00025) and a [radiomics study that predicts oxygen need in COVID-19 patients](https://doi.org/10.1109/BIBE60311.2023.00051). In 2020, the lab had already won the best paper award at the 20th IEEE BIBE, for work on telling pneumonia apart from COVID-19 in chest X-rays.

The prize money will go toward expanding the lab's high-performance servers with more advanced GPUs.
