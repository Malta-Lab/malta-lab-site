MALTA's project for automatic translation between **Brazilian Sign Language (LIBRAS)** and Portuguese received a **Google Award for Inclusion Research**, which supports research with high social impact. In 2022, only five universities in Latin America were selected.

## The challenge

Led by Professor Rodrigo Barros, the project builds neural networks that recognize signs in videos and translate them into Portuguese words, and also in the other direction. It also tackles problems shared by all sign languages: different signs with the same meaning, signs with several translations, and regional variation.

> With our research, we aim to reduce, and perhaps eliminate, communication barriers through a fully automatic method for two-way translation.
>
> — Rodrigo Barros, project lead (translated from Portuguese)

## The dataset

To train the models, the team is collecting and curating **more than 20,000 sign videos**, aiming to contribute to the largest annotated dataset of LIBRAS signs. Each video has to be checked against those already collected, because the same word can have more than one sign across the country.

The team is multidisciplinary: Professors Lucas Silveira Kupssinskü (School of Technology) and Janaína Pereira Claudio (School of Humanities), plus undergraduate research fellows and master's and PhD students. Google's support funds GPUs for training the neural networks and the software development.

## What came next

The LIBRAS research line has since produced, among other work, [LERMO](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v38i21.30384), a web game for sign recognition presented at AAAI 2024, and the paper [Isolated Sign Language Recognition in LIBRAS](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-15990-8_19) at BRACIS 2025.
